    Half an hour into reading karpathy's repo I had the though : "why build this when PyTorch already exists?" PyTorch is fast, works on tensors and a one-line .backward() that runs on CUDA and you're done. But that one line is also a black box. A micrograd rips that black box open gives a detailed undersdtanding of what's happening behind the mask.

    Quick comparison

    • PyTorch: Optimized C++/CUDA backend, tensor/array operations, automatic vectorized gradients, production-ready performance. You call .backward() and the library handles everything for you.
    • Micrograd / tiny autograd: Plain Python implementation, node-by-node and number-by-number gradient propagation, extremely slow but crystal-clear about every step of backpropagation, ideal for learning the mechanics under the hood.

    Backpropagation - in a nutshell

    At training time we measure a loss L (how wrong the model is). Backpropagation computes the partial derivatives of that loss with respect to each parameter (weights and biases) using the chain rule. For a single neuron with z = w * x + b and output y = f(z), the gradient of the loss w.r.t the weight is dL/dw = dL/dy * dy/dz * dz/dw (and dz/dw = x).

    Once we have the gradient, parameters are updated with a simple gradient-descent step: w_new = w - η * dL/dw and b_new = b - η * dL/db, where η is the learning rate, a small positive scalar that controls step size. Too large and training diverges; too small and learning is painfully slow.

    ReLU (very short)

    ReLU is the activation function ReLU(x) = max(0, x). Its derivative is 1 for positive inputs and 0 for negative inputs, which makes it simple and effective in practice.
